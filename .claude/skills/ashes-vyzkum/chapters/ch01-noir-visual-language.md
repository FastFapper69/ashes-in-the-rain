# Chapter 1: Noir visual language

## Core Idea
Noir comics and films are built from **decided black shapes and hard, motivated light**, not from "darkening" a normal picture. The method matters more than the look – imitations fail because they copy flat blacks without the compositional logic.

## Frameworks
- **Black first (Mignola)**: black masses are placed as composition, not deduced from a light; they may cover what real light would leave lit. Solid black replaces rendering and reads at a glance.
  - Balance: a black mass reads as a shape only against a white area of comparable size; few mid-tones; leave space empty.
  - Failure mode: blacks without structure look *dirty*, not dark – the eye does not know where to look.
  - Works best at night, in ruins, with small casts; poor for daylight, subtle faces, information-heavy sets.
- **Subtractive drawing (Miller, Sin City)**: draw the shadows and let the paper be the light; throw the grey away; extreme negative space; selective colour on one element. The film rule was "if it's not in the panel, it's not in the frame".
- **Film noir lighting**: low-key, one hard key light, little or no fill, light sources motivated and visible in frame (street lamps, neon); venetian-blind/gobo stripes as a shorthand for being trapped; rim light to separate a figure from a dark wall.
- **Max Payne panels**: graphic-novel panels with voice-over were chosen because they were cheaper than animated cutscenes and became the identity; Max Payne 1 baked lighting into photo textures.

## Apply to Ashes
- Gemini briefs: ask for "solid black shapes placed for composition, white paper left empty, almost no grey, one hard light source visible in the picture" instead of "dark, gritty".
- Readability of enemies against dark walls (open Gemini audit point) = rim light or a lighter wall *behind the fight*, which is exactly the noir separation trick.
- Blind/window stripes are allowed only where a window exists in the painting (our light-source rule).
- Selective colour: the user rejected extra colour ("stick to the reference") – any colour accent only as in the Gemini mockups.
- Do **not** reintroduce inked light cones or strong amber cones (user rejected both) even though noir films use hard beams.

## Anti-patterns
- Grey hatching everywhere (the classic imitation failure).
- Light with no source; glow blobs instead of shaped light.
- Daylight scenes in this style (they fight the method).

## Key Takeaways
1. Decide the blacks first; balance them with equal white.
2. Every light is a practical in the frame.
3. Separate figures by rim light / lighter background, never by soft glow.
