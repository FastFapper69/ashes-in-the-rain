# Chapter 2: Storytelling

## Core Idea
Noir games that are remembered marry a damaged narrator's inner voice with play that expresses the same character. When the player's actions and the hero's personality pull apart, the story breaks (the L.A. Noire lesson).

## Frameworks
- **Hard-boiled inner monologue (Sam Lake / Max Payne)**: pulp metaphors, a psychological thriller under the action, a protagonist deconstructed as the game goes; dark tone broken by occasional humour and meta moments. Story ideas were added and dropped as they evolved (Valkyr mutation was cut).
- **Panels as identity**: comic panels + voice were the cheap option and became the signature. MP2 added 3D cutscenes but kept the panels.
- **Role clarity (L.A. Noire critique)**: a fixed-personality hero is fine, but it must be clear what the player's interaction *means* – challenge, understanding the mystery, or choosing complicity. Interrogations were praised as a conceit and criticised as vague and repetitive (same formula each case).
- **Fragmented reveal (Katana ZERO, Hotline Miami)**: between-level scenes that reveal the plot in pieces; the gameplay loop stays brutal and short while the story carries the meaning.

## Apply to Ashes
- Our guilt/ash choices (let go vs execute) are the "complicity" channel – keep them consistent with Frank's voice: narration should react to what the player did, not ignore it.
- Inner voice at hp 1 is a good Max Payne/Sin City device – keep lines short and varied (6 now).
- Each chapter's boss should foreshadow the next chapter (one arc), each interlude reveals one piece (dream, 1931, Rosie) – no plot holes, check every new panel against earlier ones.
- Humour beats: one small dry joke per chapter lowers fatigue (Max Payne did this) – optional, must fit Frank.

## Anti-patterns
- Repeating the same scene formula every chapter.
- Hero says one thing, the player is pushed to do the opposite without the story acknowledging it.

## Key Takeaways
1. The narrator's voice is the brand – protect its consistency.
2. Make player choices visible in the story.
3. Reveal in fragments between maps; keep maps tight.
